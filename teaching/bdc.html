// Syllabus

6 CFU — M.Sc. in Computer Science, Sapienza University of Rome. Summary of the official course syllabus (A.Y. 2026/27); the complete document, including all details, is available upon request.

Learning Objectives

The course introduces big data computing as an advanced structural paradigm of modern computing platforms, at the intersection of distributed computing architectures, high-dimensional data representations, and scalable machine learning systems. Students learn how the scale-out model handles volume, velocity and variety of data, and the methodological abstractions needed where classical exact computation becomes intractable.

Prerequisites

Required: algorithms and data structures, operating systems, basics of distributed systems, and introductory machine learning mathematics (linear algebra, calculus, basic optimization).
Recommended: prior familiarity with data analytics concepts and concurrent programming abstractions.

Course Contents — 4 Modules

1. Big Data Infrastructure — scale-out storage, fault tolerance via lineage, MapReduce, memory-centric DAG execution (Spark), streaming.
2. High-Dimensional Data Representations — geometry of high-dimensional spaces, dimensionality reduction (Johnson–Lindenstrauss), columnar layouts, text/image/graph embeddings.
3. Classical Non-Learning Tasks — web-scale indexing, sampling theory, Approximate Query Processing, sub-linear sketching (HyperLogLog, Count-Min), Locality-Sensitive Hashing.
4. Scale-Out Learning Systems — large-scale ERM, parallel/asynchronous SGD, Parameter Server, Federated Learning, memory-bandwidth optimization.

Assessment Method

Research Seminar Presentation (mandatory, 100% of final grade): an oral presentation and critical seminar on a recent, high-impact research paper, chosen autonomously by the student and approved by the instructor from top-tier venues (e.g., SIGMOD, VLDB, OSDI, SOSP, NeurIPS, ICML, KDD). Minimum score 18/30 to pass; honors (30 cum laude) require exceptional mastery, deep critical insight, and flawless academic communication.

Textbooks & References

Mining of Massive Datasets (Leskovec, Rajaraman & Ullman) and Foundations of Data Science (Blum, Hopcroft & Kannan) — both available free online.
Designing Data-Intensive Applications (Kleppmann); Introduction to Information Retrieval (Manning, Raghavan & Schütze); Understanding Machine Learning (Shalev-Shwartz & Ben-David).
Plus canonical research papers referenced throughout the lecture material.

Teaching Delivery & Attendance

Purely theoretical and methodological lectures covering architectural design, mathematical proofs, and platform mechanics. No practical lab sessions, programming assignments, or supervised exercises. Attendance is highly recommended.
